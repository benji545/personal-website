Synchronization

Sixty-four oscillators, each with its own natural frequency, every one pulled toward the group's average phase with coupling strength K. Weak coupling leaves them drifting on their own. Past a critical value they lock together, the way fireflies, pacemaker cells in the heart, and generators on a power grid fall into step.

dθi/dt = ωi + K r sin(ψ − θi)

This is the Kuramoto model. For this spread of frequencies, locking starts near K ≈ 1.

Chua's circuit

The simplest electronic circuit that behaves chaotically: two capacitors, an inductor, a resistor, and one nonlinear negative resistance called a Chua diode. Turn up α, the ratio of the two capacitors, and the oscillation doubles its period, turns chaotic in one lobe, and then starts jumping unpredictably between two: the double scroll.

ẋ = α(y − x − f(x)), ẏ = x − y + z, ż = −βy
